## The brief (target 250 to 300 words)

**Story summary.** In a rural JHS with no student internet access, a small Gemma 3 1B model
runs offline on a Raspberry Pi 4 in the science classroom. Students use shared phones or an old
laptop to ask Integrated Science revision questions and receive short flashcard answers that
their teacher has checked. The question for governance is how to keep this helpful, accurate and
fair as it spreads.

**Benefits and risks.**

| Benefit or risk | Who gains or who carries it |
|---|---|
| Benefit: curriculum-matched revision without internet | Students in under-connected schools |
| Benefit: less preparation time for revision material | Teachers |
| Risk: a confident but wrong card teaches a misconception | Students; teachers who must correct it |
| Risk: over-reliance, or use as a shortcut in exams | Students; school heads and GES (exam integrity) |
| Risk: English-only answers exclude some learners | Students who learn best in Twi or other languages |
| Risk: collecting student data without consent | Students and parents |

**Governance responses.**

1. **Enforce existing law.** Follow Ghana's Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843) by collecting no
   student data at all: no accounts, no names, no stored questions linked to a person.
2. **Bright-line rules.** The tool is never used during tests or exams, and it never replaces
   the teacher. It is a revision aid only.
3. **Safeguards.**
   - Teacher review: a science teacher approves every card before students see it.
   - Error reporting: a simple paper or on-screen form lets anyone flag a wrong card; the teacher
     fixes or removes it.
   - Pilot before scaling: one teacher and one class first; scale to more schools only with
     teacher review panels and agreement from the school head and GES.

Language exclusion remains open and is listed as future work (Twi explanations).
